About

AI tooling and a shared library, built together

Flywheel is an open initiative to build AI-for-math tooling for Lean and use it to formalize computer science in a single, unified library.

Why now

AI systems for theorem proving and autoformalization are improving quickly. Agents can now write and repair Lean proofs at a scale that was out of reach a few years ago. Computer science is a natural target for them: its objects are precise, its results matter for real software, and much of it has not been formalized yet.

That work is currently split across many independent projects [TBD: examples]. Each has its own tooling, its own conventions, and its own partial library. The tools can’t share what they learn, and the libraries can’t build on each other.

  1. Fragmented toolingAgents, harnesses, and evaluation scripts are rebuilt by each project, and improvements don’t carry over.
  2. Fragmented librariesOverlapping CS material is formalized several times with incompatible encodings.
  3. No shared yardstickWithout common benchmarks, it’s hard to tell which tools actually work on CS formalization.
  4. No coordination pointThere is no single place to see what exists, what is in progress, and where new work belongs.

What this enables

For AI4Math researchers

Open tooling, a large consistent library, and shared benchmarks to train, evaluate, and compare systems on.

Result: faster, measurable progress on autoformalization.
For CS formalization projects

Automation that does the routine work, and a common core to build on instead of reinventing foundations.

Result: more coverage with less duplicated effort.
For researchers and students

Verified definitions and theorems across CS that they can cite, reuse, and extend.

Result: trustworthy building blocks for new work.
For verified software

Proven algorithms and semantics that code-level verification can rely on.

Result: a stronger link between theory and real systems.

Open by design

Flywheel is open source: tools, models where possible, library, benchmarks, and data. It is built to complement the existing Lean ecosystem, including Mathlib and CSLib, not to compete with it. [TBD: how Flywheel relates to and upstreams into existing libraries.]

The aimMake formalizing computer science fast enough that one shared, complete library becomes realistic, and make sure every project that contributes benefits from it.
